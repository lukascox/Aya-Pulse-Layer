Session pulse_20260802_201343 (20:13:44 -> 20:32:01, ~18 min, Eden on a fixed

TWO SENSORS, AND THEY DISAGREE BY ~15 C ON TRANSIENTS.

cap_poll (raw sysfs cpu_temp_mc) recorded a 78.7 C peak. The fan controller,
regulating at the same moment, read 58-63 C. It is a smoothed or different
zone and does not follow short spikes.

The whole event lasted five seconds -- only 8 of 852 cap_poll samples in the
session exceeded 70 C:

  20:15:50  cpu_temp_mc=55400  fan_duty=63
  20:15:51  cpu_temp_mc=74400  fan_duty=63

  20:15:58  cpu_temp_mc=63200  fan_duty=51    <-- PULSE reasserts 20%
  20:15:59  cpu_temp_mc=64300  fan_duty=99

What PULSE's fan loop believed at the same time:

  20:15:56 PulseFan: CUSTOM fan running (autoTdp=true smart=true temp=58 applied=20% target=20)
  20:15:58 PulseFan: CUSTOM fan running (autoTdp=true smart=true temp=63 applied=20% target=20)

TWO CONSEQUENCES.

1. The revised curve was never tested. Its knee is above 60 C on the sensor
   the curve reads, and that sensor never exceeded 67 C all session. Every one
   of the 180 decisions returned target=20, the floor -- exactly as configured,
   with no evidence either way about the sloped part of the curve.

2. PULSE undoes the vendor's thermal response. The vendor ramped to duty 163
   in reaction to a real 78.7 C reading; PULSE reasserted duty 51 three
   seconds later, on the strength of its own 63 C reading. Harmless for a
   five-second transient. Under a sustained load, PULSE would be holding the
   fan down while the chip is genuinely hot, and its own sensor would not
   report it.

This is the mechanism behind "Custom is quieter and hotter than off", recorded
in the same directory's NOTES.md -- and it is a stronger statement than the
earlier one, because it is no longer only about how the curve is configured.
