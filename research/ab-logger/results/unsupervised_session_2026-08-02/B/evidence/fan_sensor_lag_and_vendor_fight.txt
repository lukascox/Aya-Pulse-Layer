CORRECTED 2026-08-02. An earlier version of this file claimed "two sensors,
and they disagree by ~15 C". That was wrong. There is ONE sensor, both sides
read it correctly, and the apparent gap was smoothing lag on a five-second
transient. The measurement that settles it is at the bottom.

Session pulse_20260802_201343 (20:13:44 -> 20:32:01, ~18 min, Eden on a fixed
Balanced tier, AutoTDP off). Note: the Fan card was on CUSTOM with "hold
target temp" (78 C), not the curve -- see fan_hold_target_temp_at_floor.txt.

THE EVENT. cap_poll (raw sysfs cpu_temp_mc) recorded a 78.7 C peak while the
fan controller, at the same second, was working from 58-63 C:

  20:15:50  cpu_temp_mc=55400  fan_duty=63
  20:15:51  cpu_temp_mc=74400  fan_duty=63
  20:15:53  cpu_temp_mc=76400  fan_duty=63
  20:15:54  cpu_temp_mc=78300  fan_duty=63
  20:15:55  cpu_temp_mc=78700  fan_duty=163   <-- vendor ramps to ~64%
  20:15:57  cpu_temp_mc=72100  fan_duty=163
  20:15:58  cpu_temp_mc=63200  fan_duty=51    <-- PULSE reasserts 20%
  20:15:59  cpu_temp_mc=64300  fan_duty=99

  20:15:56 PulseFan: CUSTOM fan running (autoTdp=true smart=true temp=58 applied=20% target=20)
  20:15:58 PulseFan: CUSTOM fan running (autoTdp=true smart=true temp=63 applied=20% target=20)

The whole spike lasted five seconds; only 8 of 852 samples cleared 70 C.

WHY THERE IS NO SENSOR DISCREPANCY. Both sides resolve the same thermal zone
by the same rule -- first /sys/class/thermal/thermal_zoneN whose `type`
contains "cpu" -- and pulse_daemon.sh says so in a comment, deliberately
matched to SystemTuning.kt#resolveZones(). The difference is that the app side
applies an EMA (0.6 x old + 0.4 x new, ForegroundAppMonitorService.kt ~line
931) and cap_poll does not.

THE MEASUREMENT. Matching the two logs on identical timestamps across the
whole session, 140 pairs:

  median difference  -0.6 C
  p10 / p90          -1.5 C / +0.4 C
  range              -3.4 C to +6.1 C
  |difference| > 5 C  1 pair out of 140

So at steady state the fan controller's temperature IS the raw sysfs
temperature. The 15 C gap existed only during the five-second ramp, which is
what an EMA is supposed to do.

WHAT REMAINS TRUE, AT ITS CORRECTED WEIGHT. PULSE reasserted duty 51 three
seconds after the vendor ramped to 163. That is real, and it is PULSE winning
the arbitration as designed (reassert cadence FAN_RECHECK_MS = 120 ms). But it
is not evidence of a blind controller: with a 1 Hz EMA at alpha 0.4 the
smoothed value converges on a sustained change within roughly ten seconds. The
controller ignores five-second spikes and responds to real heat. For a fan,
that is the desired behaviour, not a defect.

The earlier conclusion drawn from this file -- "check which sensor
FanTempController reads before tuning the curve any further" -- is withdrawn.
It reads the right one.
